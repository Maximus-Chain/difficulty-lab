Choosing ASERT's half-life

The main report compares every candidate algorithm. This page narrows it to one decision: is ASERT with τ=24 the right replacement for DGW v3, or would LWMA-1 N=45 or a longer ASERT half-life serve Maximus better? The answer depends on how big real hashrate collapses are, who mines the chain, and how the merge-mining pools behave, so all three are measured here from the chain itself.

Research only. No change to the difficulty algorithm or to emission has been decided. Any such change would be a hard fork, announced well in advance with an activation height and an upgrade window for pools, exchanges and node operators.

Real collapses

How big do hashrate collapses get on Maximus?

Hashrate is measured hour by hour from the chain: every block's work is spread over the time it took to find, so even an hour without blocks gets an estimate. A collapse is a run of hours below half the median of the previous 24 hours, and its depth is the drop of its median hour.

Merge-mining pools

During Maximus's collapses, the pool kept mining Dash

Maximus is merge-mined on top of Dash, next to Osmium (chain ID 0x62), which is merge-mined on the same Dash work. If the dominant pool kept finding Dash and Osmium blocks while Maximus collapsed, its hashrate never left: it only stopped including Maximus.

Replaying every real collapse, plus everyday stability

Each of the chain's real collapses is simulated with its own depth and duration, and the totals are scaled to one year. That weights each collapse size by how often it really happens, instead of picking one scenario. Steady-state columns come from replaying the real hashrate; the last two come from the switching simulations of the main report.

Method

How the numbers were produced

Collapses come from the headers of every mainnet block. The pools come from getblock on every block: the coinbase scriptSig, the payout address and the AuxPoW parent block with its coinbase. The pool name is the /name/ tag pools write there; random extranonce bytes around it are ignored.

An AuxPoW parent block is usually only a share. It meets Maximus's target, not Dash's, so its own hash is not on Dash. The parent check therefore looks up the block it builds on, which must be on Dash's main chain at the parent's height minus one, in a public Dash explorer. Dash's own pool shares come from 30 Dash blocks sampled by height on each side of a collapse, so each sampled block is worth about 3 percentage points. Osmium comes from a full node and every block is counted.

Simulations use the same code as the main report: exponential block work, s of wasted work per block, and each new rule activated at a hard fork from a chain in equilibrium at today's difficulty. "Normal pace" means 24 consecutive blocks averaging under 1.5·T. "Blocks lost" are blocks a perfectly paced chain would have produced during the collapse, in hours of blocks. "Burst" counts blocks beyond the normal 50 in the first hour after the hashrate returns.
